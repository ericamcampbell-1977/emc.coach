The protocol

A lab is a place to experiment.

Coaching isn't leading. It's listening and expanding. We try things safely, learn from what happens, and keep what works.

Fig. I

Discover

We get curious about what you want and what's underneath it: the fears, blockers, and motivators shaping your choices.

Fig. II

Experiment

You try something new between sessions, a conversation, a boundary, a different choice, with no pressure to get it perfect.

Fig. III

Observe

We slow down and notice what's really happening: your patterns, your energy, what you say and what you don't.

Fig. IV

Evolve

We look at what you learned, keep what works, adjust what doesn't, and build on it. Progress you can see and measure.

Method

Integrative Relational Systems

The foundations of our course are Parts Work and Nonviolent Communication. Combined, they create Relational Integrative Systems (RIS): a way to understand what's happening inside you, how you speak, and how you show up with others.

Foundations of our course

  1. PARTS

    Parts Work

    Your mind is made of parts: the perfectionist, inner critic, or the scapegoat, and every part is trying to help. We get curious about those parts instead of fighting them, so you can lead from a calmer, clearer place.

  2. NVC

    Nonviolent Communication

    Say what you observe, feel, and need, and make a clear request, even in tense conversations. Hard things, said without attacking or collapsing.

  3. RIS

    Relational Integrative Systems

    Under stress, an old, protective part of us takes over from our wise adult. We work on staying in the center, neither one-up nor one-down, at work, at home, and with yourself.

This is coaching informed by these frameworks. They shape how I listen and ask questions. They are not therapy, and I don't use them to diagnose or treat.

The researcher

Hi, I'm Erica Campbell, and I'm so glad you're here.

I'm based in Kansas City, I'm an avid lifelong learner, and I love understanding people and helping them break down the barriers between where they are and where they want to be.

I spent more than 25 years in executive leadership. Along the way I lived through burnout and adversity, and I know how it feels when the path forward seems blocked, no matter how hard you push.

That's why I advocate for people finding their way to a higher purpose: breaking down the barriers to success, getting to the root of the patterns that keep you stuck, and moving through the fear and obstacles in the way. I believe anything in life is possible when you put your mind to it and take action.

It's also why I built my work on Parts Work and NVC, and combined them into RIS. Each one shows a different piece of the picture: what's happening inside you, how you say what you need, and how you show up with others. Together they give you a clear understanding of yourself, and a clear way forward.

My superpower: I notice what shifts. I pick up on small changes in tone, energy, and body language that others often miss. In a session, that means I notice when something lands, when you pull back, or when the real issue just surfaced, and I reflect it back so you can see it too.

Remember this: no one alive is any better than you. We all came into this world the same way, and we'll all leave it the same way. Everything in between holds infinite possibility.

Terms of the study

What coaching is (and isn't)

In plain terms: you are the expert on your life. My job is to help you think more clearly, see what's in the way, and act on what you decide. I follow the ICF Code of Ethics and Core Competencies.

Coaching isCoaching is not
Forward-focused: where you are and where you want to goTherapy or counseling, or a replacement for either
A partnership where you set the agendaDiagnosis or treatment of any mental health condition
Support to find your voice, boundaries, and next stepsLegal advice or legal representation
Confidential, within the limits of law and ICF ethicsTelling you what to do

Coaching can happen alongside therapy. If what you need is outside coaching, I'll tell you honestly and help you find the right professional. If you are in crisis: coaching is not an emergency service. In the U.S., call or text 988, or call 911.
